Anyone who watches UFC probably thinks I’m an idiot for writing this, which is completely fair. But at least hear me out.

## Fight Preview

Josh Hokit comes into the fight standing 5'11" and usually weighs in around 235 lbs. Cyril “Bon Gamin” Gane has a much larger frame at 6'4" and usually weighs in around 247 lbs.

Right off the bat, Hokit seems physically outmatched. Gane is one of the most athletically gifted fighters not just in the heavyweight division, but on the whole UFC roster. Hokit is undersized but moderately athletic. You have to respect that he was a decorated wrestler and that he played running back in the NFL.

## How Does Hokit Win?

The odds are stacked against him. He has to be wary of Gane’s knockout power. In his last fight, Gane dominated former two-division champion Alex Pereira on the feet before ending his night by KO in Round 2. Few fighters have shown they can stand and bang with Cyril Gane.

Most of Hokit’s UFC fights have ended in KOs, but he hasn’t faced anyone on Gane’s level.

So how could Hokit win this fight?

The answer is pretty simple: Josh Hokit needs to return to his roots and wrestle Cyril Gane for five rounds. The ground game is Gane’s biggest weakness. Besides his submission loss to Jon Jones, Gane lost a decision to Francis Ngannou, who wrestled his way to the win, and Alexander Volkov took Gane down pretty easily in their fights.

Hokit’s pure wrestling is probably some of the best at heavyweight right now, and I don’t think Gane is ready for what he’s going to see.

### **Give me Hokit by 48-47 decision. Book it.**
